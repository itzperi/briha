A traditional Tamil vegetarian breakfast, called tiffin, is built around fermented rice and urad dal batter or steamed rice preparations, served with chutney and sambar. It is light, quick to digest and usually finishes with a cup of filter coffee.

## The usual plates

- **Idly:** a soft steamed cake of fermented rice and urad dal, eaten with sambar and chutney.
- **Dosa:** a thin, crisp or soft crepe from the same kind of batter. Variations include plain, ghee roast, rava and uthappam.
- **Idiyappam:** steamed rice-flour string hoppers, often eaten with coconut milk or a kurma-style gravy.
- **Parotta and chapathi:** layered or flat breads served with salna, a thin spiced gravy.

## Why chutney and sambar matter

The batter is mild, so the side dishes carry the flavour. Coconut chutney is cooling, tomato or onion chutney is sharper, and sambar adds lentils and vegetables.

## Filter coffee to finish

South Indian filter coffee is brewed by dripping hot water through ground coffee in a metal filter, then mixed with hot milk and sugar.
